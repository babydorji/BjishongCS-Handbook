School History

From a humble primary school in 1997 to a thriving central school today.

1997

Damji Primary School established at the old location.

2007

Upgraded to Damji Middle Secondary School (PP–IX) and relocated to a new site.

2015

Renamed Bjishong Central School.

2017

Upgraded to Higher Secondary School.

2021

Identified as a School with SEN Program and pilot school for Bhutan Baccalaureate.

2022

Transition of Bhutan Baccalaureate (BB) program completed.

Special Programs

Holistic development through Mentorship, Reading, Driglam and Scouting.

🤝 Mentorship Program

Builds honest, trust-based relationships between students (mentees) and teachers (mentors). Focuses on individualized care to actualize innate potential across all 5 Areas of Development.

📖 DEAR Program

Drop Everything And Read — conducted twice weekly. Aims to improve reading culture, communication skills, and English/Dzongkha literacy. Includes an intensive Reading Week in September.

🏕 Scouting Program

Part of the world's largest educational youth movement (54M+ members, 216 countries). Supports wholesome development through outdoor, experiential learning and values-based activities.

🇧🇹 Driglam Namzha

Every Monday, students practice Driglam — Bhutan's code of etiquette and cultural identity — to preserve and promote the nation's unique traditions across generations.

Student Code of Conduct

All students are expected to uphold these standards at all times.

  • 1Must be in national dress in all public places.
  • 2Should not write or scribble on textbooks or library books.
  • 3Not allowed to drive within school premises.
  • 4Must wear facemask during required circumstances.
  • 5Senior students should be role models and caring to juniors.
  • 6Must follow school timing strictly; arrive at venues at least 5 minutes early.
  • 7Must wear full school uniform during school hours and activities.
  • 8Must attend all school activities: assembly, classes, games, SUPW, prayers, studies.
  • 9Must show respect to teachers, support staff and elders at all times.
  • 10Must take care of school furniture, fittings and property. Damage = full cost liability.
  • 12Strictly prohibited: smoking, chewing tobacco/doma, consuming alcohol, drugs and gambling.
  • 13Dangerous items (chains, knives, knuckles, etc.) are prohibited.
  • 19Must speak only Dzongkha or English while in school.
  • 20Minimum 90% attendance required; failing = withdrawal from examinations.
  • 26Mobile phones, music sets and electronic gadgets not allowed (subject to confiscation).
  • 29Tattooing is strictly prohibited. Penalty applies for both erasable and permanent tattoos.

School Discipline Policy

A four-level framework to manage behaviour consistently and fairly.

Level 1 — D Degree (Minor)

Includes: Absenteeism, tardiness, inappropriate haircut/dress, graffiti, chewing doma, eve-teasing, verbal harassment, indecent language.

Action: Mentor-mentee conference, undertaking letter, inform parents, record on file.

Level 2 — C Degree (Moderate)

Includes: Repeating Level 1 offences, bunking class/school, gambling, mobile phone confiscation, exam cheating, hooliganism, match-making.

Action: 5-day in-campus detention, fine Nu. 1,000 (mobile), 10% exam mark deduction, inform parents.

Level 3 — B Degree (Serious)

Includes: Repeating Level 2, gang formation, illicit relationships, theft, drinking, trespassing, physical assault without weapons.

Action: 10-day in-campus detention, call parents, counsellor referral, undertaking letter.

Level 4 — A Degree (Expulsion Risk)

Includes: Repeating Level 3, weapon-use altercations, resisting school authority, bullying with weapons, sexual misconduct, vandalism, drug supply/use.

Action: Immediate parent notification, school relocation in consultation with Dzongkhag, TC issuance if no school accepts.

📱 Mobile Phone Policy

  • 1Submit phones to warden/matron on day of arrival.
  • 2Mentors may allow use at least once a month for educational purposes.
  • 5Found in possession without submitting: Nu. 1,000 fine; confiscated until end of term.
  • 7Repeat offence: Nu. 1,500 fine; phone returned only at end of year. Non-payment cancels TC and Progress Report.

Student Health & PHC Protocol

Clear procedures to ensure every student receives timely, appropriate care.

3.1 Minor Health Concerns

Headaches, stomach pain, minor wounds — first aid provided on campus by the School Health In-charge.

3.2 Scheduled PHC Visits

Moderate medical attention: referred to PHC on Thursdays after lunch, escorted by the school caregiver.

3.3 Emergency Cases

Serious illness: student immediately escorted to PHC by Warden/Matron with the school driver.

3.5 Bed Rest

Permitted only upon recommendation from a health professional (HA/Sister).

3.6 & 3.7 PHC Cards

Cards collected during morning interval from respective health in-charges. Must be returned after the PHC visit.

3.9 CID Requirement

Students must bring their CID number when visiting the PHC to enter in the system.

Awards & Recognition

Celebrating excellence across academics, leadership and character.

🌟 Best Student of the Year

For graduating Class XII students. Criteria: academic excellence (above 65%), school representation, leadership, extracurricular participation, character & integrity, attendance.

🏅 Best Captain Award

For any student leader. Criteria: academics (must pass term exam), exceptional leadership, significant contribution, extracurricular participation, integrity and punctuality.

📜 Academic Awards

Certificates for class toppers (Mid-Term), level toppers (Trial/Annual), and students scoring 90%+ in written exams for both terms.

🎖 Student Leadership Certificate

Awarded to all captains who meet the leadership, contribution and conduct criteria throughout the academic year.
